### W2 — Western UP food-safety crackdown: Bareilly, Moradabad and Muzaffarnagar
**Region/time allocation:** Western UP, 1:45  
**What is reported:** UP FSDA's pre-Diwali drive reportedly inspected 1,133 locations and collected 935 samples from 26 Oct–3 Nov; 54 quintals of food products worth about ₹1.32 crore were seized and 27 establishments' licences suspended. HT and TOI report that 32 mobile food-safety squads carried out special operations in Bareilly and Moradabad. Reported examples include unhygienic sweet-making/storage conditions in Bareilly and expired ingredients, inedible colours and rodent traces in Moradabad. In Muzaffarnagar, Jagran and TOI report that about 220 kg of sweets were destroyed after flies/insects were found.
**Why it matters:** Directly relevant to families buying sweets, milk products and festival food.
**Editorial handling:** Clearly distinguish inspection findings, notices, licence suspensions and laboratory-confirmed adulteration. Do not call every seized item “poisonous” or say every sample failed. In Muzaffarnagar, one local report specifies rasgullas while another uses a general description of sweets; safest wording is “about 220 kg of sweets”. Do not name or visually shame a shop; mention it only if an official public order and legal review support doing so. Add a consumer tip: check sealed packaging, date, FSSAI licence details and retain the bill; report suspected unsafe food to the state food-safety authority.
**Sources:** Hindustan Times, 3 Nov 2026, 19:55 IST; Times of India, 4 Nov 2026, 01:06 IST; The Statesman, 3 Nov 2026; Dainik Jagran, 4 Nov 2026, 13:22 IST; TOI Muzaffarnagar follow-up, 5 Nov 2026.

### N1 — Weak monsoon, winter sowing and food prices; food vs fuel in ethanol policy
**Region/time allocation:** India-wide, 1:20  
**What is confirmed/reported:** Reuters reports that weak monsoon soil moisture is constraining winter sowing and putting pressure on supplies of wheat, pulses, sugar, onions, milk and poultry. Reuters says monsoon rainfall was nearly 13% below normal and corn/sugar prices had risen sharply; those commodity movements are reported market estimates, not a guarantee of a uniform retail price rise in every mandi. Official September CPI data show retail inflation at 5.54% and food inflation at 7.74%; food inflation was 8.06% rural and 7.18% urban. A separate Reuters report says ethanol blending could fall from the 20% level to roughly 15–17% in the supply year that began this month because of feedstock shortages; this is an industry estimate, not a confirmed government target.
**Why it matters:** Links farmers' sowing decisions, mandi supply, household grocery bills and petrol-blending policy.
**Editorial handling:** Explain the chain: low soil moisture → sowing/yield risk → supply/price pressure. Do not say all groceries have already risen by a specific amount or that petrol prices must rise. Label the 15–17% blending figure as an estimate.
**Sources:** Reuters, 3 Nov 2026; Reuters, 4 Nov 2026, 08:32 IST; PIB/MoSPI official September CPI release, 12 Oct 2026.

### N2 — Energy-drink labels: FSSAI and major brands' court dispute
**Region/time allocation:** India-wide, 0:55  
**What is reported:** In court filings reported by Reuters on 4 November, FSSAI argued that high-caffeine drinks marketed as “energy drinks” could mislead consumers and raised concerns about caffeine, sugar and taurine. PepsiCo and Monster are challenging the regulator's labelling action and say they were not given adequate notice/hearing. The Delhi High Court's earlier interim order allowed sale of existing stock carrying the label but did not permit new manufacture under that label. The case was listed for a further hearing on 5 November.
**Why it matters:** A familiar product-category dispute involving consumer information and youth-facing marketing.
**Editorial handling:** This is a labelling/legal dispute, not a court finding that the products are poisonous or that every can is unsafe. Do not announce the 5 November hearing result in this 02:04 IST snapshot; refresh later. Attribute the regulator's allegation and the companies' challenge separately.
**Sources:** Reuters, 4 Nov 2026, 17:10 IST; LiveLawBiz, 7 Oct 2026, Delhi High Court interim-order report; Economic Times reprint of Reuters, 4 Nov 2026 (same underlying Reuters reporting, not an independent confirmation).

### N3 — CERT-In warns of AI-assisted attacks on banks and fintech firms
**Region/time allocation:** India-wide, 1:00  
**What is confirmed:** CERT-In's critical advisory CIAD-2026-0058, issued 4 November, describes an active and escalating AI-assisted campaign against banks, NBFCs, digital lenders, fintech/payment firms and crypto-service providers. It names the threat actor “CRAFTY FALCON” and says attacks exploit exposed systems and payment/disbursement APIs. Reuters and PTI/Mint also report an I4C warning to financial institutions. Reuters says I4C suggested safeguards including daily/per-transaction limits, beneficiary-name verification and monitoring unusual transaction speeds.
**Why it matters:** Online banking, loan disbursals and payment infrastructure affect ordinary customers.
**Editorial handling:** The advisory is aimed at financial institutions; do not imply that every bank account is compromised or that a specific viewer's money is at risk. Give practical advice: never share OTP/PIN, verify the payee before authorising, keep bank apps updated, and report a financial cyber-fraud immediately through 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in.
**Sources:** CERT-In official advisory CIAD-2026-0058, 4 Nov 2026; Reuters, 4 Nov 2026, 19:04 IST; Mint/PTI, 4 Nov 2026, 20:45 IST; I4C portal.

### N4 — Drug-quality draft: immediate licence suspension proposed after failed government tests
**Region/time allocation:** India-wide, 0:55  
**What is proposed:** A draft amendment dated 28 October and published on 4 November proposes immediate suspension of the manufacturing licence for the relevant drug when a government analyst certifies specified quality failures such as impurity, sterility, assay or dissolution problems. The licence would remain suspended until root-cause analysis and corrective/preventive action are completed and verified. Reuters reports a 30-day public-comment period; the draft says it would take effect only after final Gazette publication.
**Why it matters:** Medicine quality is a direct public-health concern.
**Editorial handling:** This is a proposal, not an already-in-force rule. Do not say licences are being automatically cancelled today. Explain that suspension is proposed pending investigation and verified corrective action.
**Sources:** Reuters, 4 Nov 2026, 22:58 IST; Gazette Tracker transcript of Ministry of Health and Family Welfare draft G.S.R. 956(E), published 4 Nov 2026; CDSCO Gazette Notifications page for related 28 Oct draft-rule publication context.

### N5 — Delhi-NCR Diwali: Supreme Court sets green-cracker time windows
**Region/time allocation:** India/NCR, 0:55  
**What is confirmed:** The Supreme Court's 29 October order, made public on 2 November, permits only certified green crackers in Delhi-NCR between 6–7 am and 8–10 pm on 7 and 8 November. Fireworks are prohibited within 300 metres of hospitals, nursing homes, health centres, old-age homes, educational institutions and notified silence zones. The full ban returns from 9 November; barium-containing, non-approved and joined-series crackers are not allowed.
**Why it matters:** Useful for families planning Diwali and for public health/air-quality awareness, including NCR residents.
**Editorial handling:** Emphasise that permission is limited by dates, timings, certification and no-cracker zones. Do not imply unrestricted sale or use. Ask viewers to follow local enforcement directions.
**Sources:** ThePrint/PTI, 2 Nov 2026, 15:47 IST; Indian Express, 3 Nov 2026, 09:13 IST; Economic Times Government/PTI, 2 Nov 2026, 21:08 IST.
