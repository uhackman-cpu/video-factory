### W1 — Moradabad: Delhi–Bareilly roadways bus catches fire, all 55 passengers escape
A Bareilly-depot AC roadways bus travelling from Delhi to Bareilly caught fire near the Dalpatpur overbridge on the Delhi–Lucknow National Highway at about 9 pm on 4 November. Dainik Jagran and Amar Ujala both report that all 55 passengers were evacuated safely and two fire tenders attended. Amar Ujala quotes the Chief Fire Officer attributing the fire to a rear tyre burst; no final engineering or forensic report was located.
**Audience angle:** Passenger safety, bus maintenance and what to do when a vehicle catches fire.
**Verification:** Two independent local reports corroborate the safe evacuation and fire response. Attribute the tyre-burst explanation to the Chief Fire Officer; do not call it a final technical finding. Do not use passengers' panic footage unless rights and consent are established.
**Time:** 0:45  
**Sources:** W1-S1, W1-S2

### W2 — Bareilly, Moradabad and Muzaffarnagar: festive sweet-shop safety inspections
UP FSDA's pre-Diwali campaign inspected 1,133 locations and collected 935 samples between 26 October and 3 November; reports say around 54 quintals of food products worth about ₹1.32 crore were seized and 27 establishment licences suspended statewide. The drive deployed 32 mobile squads in Bareilly and Moradabad. Local findings reported include unhygienic production and mouldy ghevar in Bareilly, expired ingredients/inedible colours/rodent contamination in Moradabad, and about 220 kg of rasgullas destroyed in Muzaffarnagar after flies were found.
**Audience angle:** How families can check packaging, manufacturing dates and shop hygiene before buying sweets.
**Caution:** Seized goods and collected samples are not automatically proof that every product was adulterated; lab findings and final orders must be distinguished from inspection observations. Use the named premises only with the exact attributed inspection report.
**Time:** 1:00  
**Sources:** W2-S1 to W2-S5

### W3 — Sambhal: ₹99,000 allegedly lost while trying to cancel an online order
A school principal in Asmoli reportedly contacted what he believed was customer care after a delayed order. He was sent a scanner/QR code to complete cancellation; about ₹99,000 was then debited. He filed an online complaint. The local report quotes the cyber police station in-charge advising the complainant to visit the local police station with evidence.
**Audience angle:** A practical warning: never scan a QR code or share credentials to receive a refund; use the official app's help section. For financial cyber fraud, call 1930 immediately and report at cybercrime.gov.in.
**Caution:** This is a reported victim complaint, not evidence of a breach or wrongdoing by Flipkart. The impersonator's identity and recovery status were not confirmed in the report.
**Time:** 0:45  
**Sources:** W3-S1, W3-S2

## India / wider UP — 8 main stories (5:00)

### N1 — Food inflation and weak soil moisture put winter crops under pressure
Reuters on 3 November reported that a weak monsoon has left soil too dry for some winter planting and could tighten supplies of wheat, rice, pulses, sugar, onions, milk and poultry. The official September CPI release reported headline inflation at 5.54% and food inflation at 7.74%; food inflation was 8.06% in rural areas and 7.18% in urban areas. The onion sub-group recorded 108.09% year-on-year inflation in September, while Uttar Pradesh's headline CPI inflation was 6.08%. These are September CPI rates, not today's quoted retail prices.
**Treatment:** Explain the possible chain from low soil moisture to sowing/yield risk and household prices. These are risks and national CPI figures, not proof that every local mandi or shop has already raised prices.
**Time:** 0:55  
**Sources:** N1-S1, N1-S2

### N2 — Cyclone Murasu: Andhra begins recovery after crop and housing losses
The latest confirmed preliminary figures located by 05:59 IST were reported by Andhra Pradesh State Disaster Management Authority Managing Director Prakhar Jain: at least two deaths, crops damaged across 52,459 hectares, 312 houses affected, estimated losses of about ₹344 crore and 12,230 people in relief centres. These figures are preliminary and were reported on 3–4 November; a newer verified official damage assessment was not located before the research cut-off. IMD said the severe cyclone weakened to a depression over east Telangana and adjoining Coastal Andhra Pradesh by 5:30 am on 4 November and was expected to weaken further into a well-marked low-pressure area.
**Treatment:** Focus on farmers, electricity restoration, roads and families in relief centres. Clearly label crop-loss and monetary figures as preliminary; do not present the cyclone as still a severe cyclonic storm.
**Time:** 0:55  
**Sources:** N2-S1, N2-S2, N2-S3, N2-S4

### N3 — Record gold prices are changing festive buying
The World Gold Council's India Q3 report says domestic gold prices averaged about 45% higher year-on-year. Q3 demand was 162.5 tonnes, down 29% year-on-year; jewellery demand fell 20% to about 100 tonnes and bar/coin demand fell 40% to 55 tonnes. The WGC's 2026 full-year outlook is 625–675 tonnes, with high prices and rural affordability constraints limiting demand despite festive and wedding-season support.
**Treatment:** Explain why households may buy lighter jewellery, exchange old gold or delay purchases. Do not claim that all Indian families have stopped buying gold.
**Time:** 0:35  
**Sources:** N3-S1, N3-S2, N3-S3

### N4 — ₹14,500 crore plan to store renewable electricity
The Union Cabinet approved a ₹14,500-crore viability-gap funding scheme for 92 GWh of grid-scale storage between FY 2026-27 and FY 2030-31: 60 GWh pumped hydro, 30 GWh battery storage and 2 GWh emerging technologies. The official note estimates total project investment of ₹97,600 crore and requires at least 40% domestic content for battery-storage projects.
**Treatment:** Explain simply: store extra solar/wind electricity and use it when generation falls; this is a scheme approval, not an immediate household bill reduction.
**Time:** 0:35  
**Sources:** N4-S1, N4-S2, N4-S3

### N5 — ₹2,814 crore scheme for lithium and nickel processing
The Cabinet approved a five-year capital-support scheme with a ₹2,814-crore outlay. It provides a 30% capital subsidy for four plants: two lithium-processing plants and two nickel-processing plants. The policy aims to develop domestic processing and reduce reliance on imported refined materials; cobalt may be recovered as a nickel-production by-product.
**Treatment:** Connect batteries, electric vehicles, electronics and defence supply chains. Avoid claiming that India has already become self-sufficient.
**Time:** 0:35  
**Sources:** N5-S1, N5-S2

### N6 — I4C warns banks and fintech firms about AI-assisted cyberattacks
An I4C/CERT-In advisory made public on 4 November warns financial institutions that attackers are using AI agents to exploit unpatched software and misconfigured systems, then target payment APIs and automated fund-disbursement systems. The advisory recommends tighter API inventories, disabling unused interfaces, transaction limits, beneficiary-name checks and reporting incidents to CERT-In/RBI within six hours.
**Treatment:** Make clear that the advisory is directed at financial institutions; it does not say every bank account or customer is compromised. Add a short consumer reminder: never share OTP/PIN, and call 1930 immediately after a financial cyber fraud.
**Time:** 0:35  
**Sources:** N6-S1, N6-S2, N6-S3

### N7 — India win rain-hit first ODI against New Zealand
India beat New Zealand by five wickets under DLS in Auckland on 4 November. New Zealand were all out for 185; India reached 143/5 in 22.4 overs against a revised target of 143 in 32 overs. Virat Kohli scored 74 off 51 balls and Arshdeep Singh took four wickets. This is a 1–0 lead in a five-match series, not a series win.
**Treatment:** A brief sports lift; keep the result and revised DLS target clear.
**Time:** 0:40  
**Sources:** N7-S1, N7-S2, N7-S3

### N8 — Fast Track Special Courts scheme extended to 2031
The Cabinet approved a five-year extension of the Fast Track Special Courts scheme for rape and POCSO cases, with a total outlay of ₹3,788.04 crore. The plan provides for expansion from 779 functional courts to up to 850; it does not mean 850 new courts are already operating. The official release reports 4,02,457 cases disposed of since the scheme began in 2019.
**Treatment:** Public-interest update on cases involving women and children; explain that actual speed of justice depends on implementation and court staffing.
**Time:** 0:40  
**Sources:** N8-S1, N8-S2, N8-S3
